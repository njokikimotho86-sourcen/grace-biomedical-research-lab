Improving smart glasses by merging reality visualisation through Help Lightning

Research problem

Many hospitals and clinics, especially outside major cities, do not have specialists or experienced engineers on site. When a complex procedure or equipment fault arises, staff often wait for an expert to travel. The project explored how smart glasses could bring that expert to the bedside virtually.

Research question

Can a remote expert, seeing exactly what an on-site health worker sees, guide them step by step through a task in real time?

Solution

The team integrated Help Lightning remote-assistance software with smart glasses. The wearer streams their point of view; the remote expert sees the scene, points, draws and guides, and that guidance is merged into the wearer's view. The working system was demonstrated live to the assessment panel.

System architecture (original project)

The wearer's view is streamed through the Help Lightning session; the expert joins from a phone or computer and their drawings appear in the wearer's display.

Limitations

  • Depends on network bandwidth and latency, which vary widely between Kenyan facilities.
  • Commercial software licence; cost and data-governance questions for public hospitals.
  • Patient privacy when streaming clinical scenes needs consent and policy controls.
  • Evaluated as a student demonstration, not a clinical trial.

What has been added since

The remote support simulator on this site is new work, built to show the workflow (equipment identification, annotation, guided troubleshooting, session reports) without the commercial platform. It does not stream real video between people.
