Medical device innovation lab

Affordable, maintainable devices for African health facilities, documented to an engineering standard from the first sketch.

Prototypes are experimental. Nothing in this lab is a clinically approved or certified medical device. In Kenya, medical devices are regulated by the Pharmacy and Poisons Board; any clinical use would need regulatory review first.

Engineering documentation template

Every prototype is documented with the same twelve sections. Copy the template below into a new project file.

  1. Problem statement: who is affected, where, how often, and current workaround.
  2. User needs: in the users' words (clinician, technician, patient).
  3. Engineering requirements: measurable, each traced to a user need.
  4. Design: concepts considered and why one was chosen.
  5. CAD: software, files, revision history, drawings.
  6. Materials: selection, biocompatibility needs, cleaning and sterilisation compatibility.
  7. Electronics: schematic, power source, isolation, components (or N/A).
  8. Prototype development: build method, settings, iterations.
  9. Testing: test plan, pass criteria, results with evidence.
  10. Safety: hazards, risk controls, residual risks (ISO 14971 style).
  11. Limitations: what it cannot do, and where it must not be used.
  12. Future development: next iteration, validation, regulatory path.
