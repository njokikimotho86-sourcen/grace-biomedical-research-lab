Finite element analysis of wear and fatigue at the hip implant head–stem interface

Software: CATIA V5, ANSYS Workbench 2024 R2

ProblemQuestionLiteratureMethodImplementationResultsLimitationsReferences

Research problem

In total hip replacement the modular connection between the femoral head and the stem taper is exposed to repeated loading and small relative movements. Wear particles released from this taper can cause inflammation, bone loss (osteolysis) and, in severe cases, revision surgery.

Research question

Can the wear and fatigue trends reported in the finite element study by Belwanshi, Jayaswal and Aherwar (2021) be reproduced independently, and where they differ, can the differences be explained?

Objectives

  1. Rebuild the implant from published dimensions in CATIA V5, with head and stem as separate bodies.
  2. Establish a mesh-independent ANSYS model at the head–taper contact.
  3. Compare three head–stem material pairs under three daily activities.
  4. Estimate linear and volumetric wear (modified Archard) and fatigue life and safety factor (stress–life with Goodman correction).
  5. Document every assumption so differences from the published study can be traced.

Background and literature

Hip contact forces during routine activities were measured in vivo with instrumented implants by Bergmann et al. (2001); those standardised loads are the basis of the load cases here. Archard's law (1953) relates wear volume to load and sliding distance and, in its modified form, gives linear wear as wear coefficient × contact pressure × sliding distance. Fatigue was assessed with the stress–life method and the Goodman mean-stress correction as presented in standard machine-design texts (Budynas and Nisbett). The target study is Belwanshi, Jayaswal and Aherwar (2021).

Methodology

1. Geometry

Implant rebuilt in CATIA V5 from published dimensions; every assumed dimension logged in a CAD construction record.

2. Mesh

Quadratic tetrahedral mesh, refined at the taper. Three-level independence study with a below-5% change criterion.

3. Loads and materials

Three activities × three material pairs = nine cases, each with its own run sheet.

4. Solver

Frictional contact with augmented Lagrange; force and displacement convergence checked for every case.

5. Wear

Modified Archard: linear wear = contact pressure × sliding distance × wear coefficient; volumetric wear over the contact area.

6. Fatigue

Stress–life with Goodman correction to estimate fatigue life and safety factor per case.

Technical implementation and rigour

The study kept a full reproducibility record: an uncertainty register, a simulation run sheet for every case, and a structured comparison of trends against the published study. The interactive biomechanics lab on this site re-implements the closed-form parts of the method (neck stress, Goodman, Archard) so that readers can explore sensitivities; it is a teaching companion to the ANSYS model, not a replacement.

Limitations

  • Geometry rebuilt from published dimensions; any undocumented features of the original model may differ.
  • Static peak loads per activity, not full gait-cycle loading.
  • Wear coefficient and material data taken from literature; results scale directly with the chosen coefficient.
  • No bone, cement or patient-specific anatomy; distal stem fixed.
  • Computational results only; no experimental validation.
